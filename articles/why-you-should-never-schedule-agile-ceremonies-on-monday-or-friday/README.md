**TL;DR:** Schedule your agile ceremonies on Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday to sidestep rescheduling, limited attendance, and/or skipping ceremonies all together.

## It's Monday

Think about your typical Monday morning: you're dusting off the weekend and gearing up for the week ahead. Everyone is getting their bearings and figuring out where things left off. Add in a holiday or an extended weekend, and it's easy to see why Mondays can have limited attendance and low team energy during these cornerstone agile activities.

## Friday, Friday

With the weekend on the horizon, we start to watch the clock and anticipate what we'll do after we sign out. At the close of the week, the team's mindset is shifting towards personal time and away from a long week of hard work. Everyone is ready for a break and attention to detail is waning. If team members are traveling or people need to leave early, attendance on Friday can be limited and less productive.

![A week laid out on a paper planner](calendar.jpg)

## So, why do Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday hit differently?

You survived Monday and your team is fully in the groove — everyone's present, ready to dive deep, and collaborate with a fresh perspective. Teams are more likely to be in full attendance, fully engaged, and more receptive to the complexities of the work.

These days offer a sweet spot for productivity and focus, with the team settled into the rhythm of the workweek. Mid-week ceremonies also offer practical flexibility. In the event of a public holiday, mid-week scheduling is less disruptive and ensures that ceremonies can proceed without significant participation gaps or compressing the process unnaturally.

Choosing Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday for your agile ceremonies isn't just about optimizing schedules — it's about aligning the natural workflow and energy levels of the team, ensuring that every ceremony is as productive and engaging as possible. Scheduling activities midweek allows teams to foster a more effective workflow that results in better outcomes for both projects and people.
